# Motion

A library of the scientific foundations of motion.

**Scope:** conceptual and mathematical structures enabling motion to be defined, compared, and known; their limits in physical and perceptual frameworks.

**Language:** English, with standard terminology and notation. This edition translates the original Indonesian foundation without changing its scientific scope.

## Main findings

Motion's foundations are better presented as conditional dependencies than an absolute list of natural constituents. State distinctions, spatial arrangements, temporal order, and comparison rules play different roles. Metrics, continuity, derivatives, inertial frames, and dynamical laws add structure; they are not all implicit in “motion”.

The six core files organize those roles scientifically. Their number does not imply six natural constituents, ontological independence, or proved universal minimality.

## Reading scientific status

- **Definition** specifies usage within a stated framework.
- **Mathematical result** follows from assumptions; it is not automatically a natural law.
- **Established within a framework** identifies standard theory or scoped empirical findings.
- **Model assumption** is an addition that can be assessed or replaced.
- **Conceptual synthesis** connects knowledge without claiming a new theorem.
- **Open** marks issues not resolved here.

Core contains scientific substance. Organization, scope boundaries, and review findings belong to Structure and Research.

Velocity, acceleration, force laws, conservation, and advanced physics appear only as needed to explain foundational limits. There is no catalogue of animation, simulation, video generation, software, or Graph integration techniques.

Compilation and source-check date: **2 November 2026**. This is a revisable, focused synthesis, not proof that every ontological question about motion is settled.
