<a id="social-story-editing"></a>
## Story & Editing

**Inputs:** Convert subject matter into a coherent, engaging short-form viewing sequence. Inputs come from the shared project contracts and connected technical owners.

**Decision:** Resolve what viewers must understand, feel or notice next. Choose to retain performance, show evidence/context, insert a metaphor, isolate text or change viewpoint from the narrative change. Use Editorial-Reasoning.md to connect those choices; no reference style, transition quota or fixed pacing is required.

**Expected mechanism:** Convert subject matter into a coherent, engaging short-form viewing sequence.

**Tradeoff:** A dramatic hook promises a different subject than the actual explanation.

**Verification:** Play the cut without explanatory commentary and record the understood message, missed beats and redundant shots.

**Output:** beat map, shot/cut plan, time-coded editorial decisions.

**Recovery:** Rewrite the hook or reorder the first explanatory beat; preserve the intended claim.

### Craft practice

- Assign inserts a role: evidence, context, illustration, counterpoint or atmosphere. Preserve provenance and do not imply that decorative footage proves a factual claim.
- Let meaningful expression or gesture complete when appropriate; cutaways can carry a narrative change while the speaker remains a continuity anchor.
- Choose a caption-led, performance-led or image-led beat deliberately. Coordinate changes in text, framing and treatment so their combined emphasis serves the same idea.

## Animated Subtitles & Captions

**Inputs:** Deliver accessible, synchronized, legible spoken content with optional expressive emphasis. Inputs come from the shared project contracts and connected technical owners.

**Decision:** Resolve whether text is a complete caption, selected emphasis or an independent text scene. Group meaning before choosing replacement, accumulation or another reveal. Preserve exact language and timing provenance; choose hierarchy and emphasis from semantics rather than styling every word identically. Read Expressive-Text.md and route independent text scenes to Kinetic Typography.

**Expected mechanism:** Deliver accessible, synchronized, legible spoken content with optional expressive emphasis.

**Tradeoff:** Captions are visually impressive but mistimed or incomplete.

**Verification:** Check approved text, qualifiers, speaker/phrase boundaries and actual speech alignment before claiming sync. Inspect final phrase layout, transient blur/scale, projected bounds and encoded mobile readability. Sampled frames leave timing and audiovisual quality unverified.

**Output:** Role-labeled caption groups with semantic spans, cue provenance, stable layout anchors and animated bounds

**Recovery:** Correct text/alignment first, then simplify or regroup the composition, restage the block and invalidate only affected cue/layout/render ranges.

### Craft practice

- Plan the final semantic group and its anchor before staged reveals when stable layout is desired. Allow intentional dynamic reflow with explicit bounds.
- Use size, weight, color, spacing, placement or a held state as alternative emphasis channels. Preserve negation and small linking words; avoid effect stacking without a reason.
- Keep captions separate from decorative keywords and provide complete captions when required. Use readable phrases when word-by-word replacement would obscure syntax.
- Allocate entry, readable state and exit from actual reading and speech needs. Motion blur is not a substitute for a legible hold; do not invent word timestamps.
- Keep a coherent local vocabulary while permitting new text roles. A source handwriting style, font, color accent or layout is not a universal preset.

## Kinetic Typography & Lyric Scenes

**Inputs:** Turn language into visual scenes, not merely moving text. Inputs come from the shared project contracts and connected technical owners.

**Decision:** Choose whether language is supporting information or the visual scene. When it becomes the scene, construct relationships among words, space, imagery and meaning. A code-like interface, hand lettering, spatial phrase, diagram or another medium is available if it serves the project; retain the approved semantic mapping and role.

**Expected mechanism:** Turn language into visual scenes, not merely moving text.

**Tradeoff:** The metaphor or deformation destroys readability before the phrase is understood.

**Verification:** Inspect the complete phrase, meaningful intermediate states, glyph correspondence and actual reading speed. Distinguish decorative syntax from verbatim text. Ensure representation changes do not alter qualifiers or imply a working product.

**Output:** text-scene plan, references, compositional states, animation timelines.

**Recovery:** Hold a readable state, simplify deformation and re-evaluate the entry/exit.

### Craft practice

- Choose the reading order and complete readable state before choosing letter behavior. Type-on should represent input or reveal intent rather than decorate every sentence.
- Preserve correspondence across icon, path and word when it helps meaning; color, position, shape or rhythm may carry the link.
- Separate text appearance from camera movement. Prefer a still reading interval when movement competes with understanding; check spacing and line breaks through the whole reveal.
- Compose connector words and focal words as one phrase with deliberate scale and spacing; reserve readable states rather than treating all words as independent effects.
- Text may alternate with footage or persist over a montage to bridge changing images. Choose continuity or reset by meaning, not a fixed sequence.
- For code/interface metaphors, separate text/semantic layers, panel state, internal viewport motion and any filmed screen perspective. A creative visual representation need not be executable code.
- Expressive irregularity should be controlled: keep useful local anchors and deterministic variation; do not add frame-random jitter solely to suggest handwriting.

## Camera, Zoom & Transition Language

**Inputs:** Direct viewer attention and connect scenes through comprehensible movement. Inputs come from the shared project contracts and connected technical owners.

**Decision:** Is movement serving a reveal, emphasis, spatial continuity, or scene change? Maintain focal continuity and avoid accidental crop of subjects/text.

**Expected mechanism:** Direct viewer attention and connect scenes through comprehensible movement.

**Tradeoff:** Camera movement cuts off protected text or produces an unintended spatial jump.

**Verification:** Inspect maximal crops and transition boundary frames while following the same subject.

**Output:** movement trajectories and shot transitions with entry/exit states.

**Recovery:** Modify framing and incoming/outgoing states together; rerun layout checks.

### Craft practice

- Declare whether a visible change comes from a camera, a group transform or internal object motion; judge the screen result without pretending the source technique is known.
- Choose a focal anchor and protect it across pan, scale and the next shot. Recompute text bounds during the full movement.
- Coordinate the handoff with Attention Direction and Motion Choreography. Fast zoom, slow drift and a static view are equally valid when appropriate.
- Separate outer frame, inset footage and text coordinates through aspect/crop changes. Caption anchors may remain in screen space or move with the footage by design.
- Coordinate reframing with emphasis, gesture and readable states. A push-in is an option; rapid zooming or simulated depth is not required for impact.

## Color Grading & Visual Look

**Inputs:** Preserve visible quality while giving footage and graphics a consistent emotional character. Inputs come from the shared project contracts and connected technical owners.

**Decision:** Interpret the source first, correct exposure/balance where needed, match only the relationships that need continuity, then choose the creative look and output treatment. Protect meaningful skin/product information and overlay contrast. Distinct footage roles may deliberately retain different looks; do not apply an inferred LUT from a finished example.

**Expected mechanism:** Preserve visible quality while giving footage and graphics a consistent emotional character.

**Tradeoff:** An intended look crushes detail or makes graphics conflict with footage.

**Verification:** Compare adjacent shots, representative skin/product detail, shadow/highlight separation, saturation, gradients and actual caption/background combinations after output conversion. Record qualitative source observations separately from measured settings or a verified color pipeline.

**Output:** shot-matched correction and look parameters tested in the rendered output.

**Recovery:** Separate source correction from the creative look; retest the output conversion.

### Craft practice

- Use value separation and warm/cool relationships as adjustable choices. Keep exact hues, contrast curves, saturation and texture in the project.
- Separate overlay rendering from the footage grade; verify any global post-treatment also applied to text. Moving backgrounds can defeat a contrast check made on one still.
- Declare scoped grayscale, wash, glow or archival differences when they serve a beat. Preserve context rather than making every source cosmetically identical.
- Do not attribute a look to grading alone: lighting, capture, set, wardrobe and compression may contribute. The visible export cannot establish the original log profile or numeric correction.

### Craft practice
