# Editorial Reasoning

Use these questions to choose and connect editing techniques. Begin from the current meaning, audience, media and project identity. A supplied example contributes possibilities and counterexamples. It does not set a required look, pace, caption format or effect density. Read [Creative Learning](Creative-Learning.md) when integrating a lesson and [Expressive Text](Expressive-Text.md) when language carries visual structure.

## Resolve the communication problem

Identify the smallest meaningful change: a new idea, a response, evidence, contrast, atmosphere or resolution. Ask what the next visible event contributes. Keep a performance when expression or gesture carries the idea; cut when the information or perspective needs to change. Complexity, novelty and frequent cuts are not completion criteria by themselves.

| Need | Candidate construction | Useful check and alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follow spoken meaning | A readable caption with stable grouping | Compare with actual speech; a conventional phrase subtitle may be clearer than isolated words. |
| Emphasize a turning point | A selected scale/weight/color change, a held shot or a changed framing | Protect qualifiers and context; emphasis can be achieved through a pause instead of a larger word. |
| Explain an abstract relationship | A diagram, metaphor, text scene or cutaway | State what information it adds; return to the performance if the metaphor adds no clarity. |
| Give context or evidence | Source footage, an archival insert or a document | Record its role and provenance; a decorative insert must not masquerade as evidence. |
| Let a reaction matter | An unhurried reaction or a wider/tighter composition | Preserve the relevant gesture and eyeline; a still frame or a direct cut may serve better than a zoom. |
| Create a lyrical or emotional interval | Spatial typography, atmosphere, montage or controlled silence | Preserve the intended meaning; do not turn all speech into decorative text. |
| Connect changing imagery | A repeated phrase, spatial anchor, gesture, shape or tonal relationship | Maintain the chosen invariant rather than forcing identical color or crop onto every image. |

These are conditional choices, not a fixed production sequence. Use only the questions that resolve the actual decision.

## Allocate visual emphasis

Choose the current dominant event and assign supporting roles to text, performance, camera and optical effects. Coordinate important changes through Attention Direction and Motion Choreography. When several layers need to change together, share an event cue; when they compete, stagger or simplify them. A subtle caption may let a face lead; a text-only frame may let a phrase lead. Neither mode is a universal default.

Plan the transition of meaning as well as pixels. Preserve a phrase, an anchor or a visual attribute across changing footage when useful. Deliberate discontinuity is valid when it communicates a new thought. Do not keep every continuity cue merely to make the cut invisible.

## Choose a visual look by relationships

Separate source interpretation and correction, shot matching, creative look, graphics treatment and output conversion. Protect meaningful skin/product detail and the contrast needed by overlays. Match adjacent shots only to the degree their role requires: an archive, a memory and a current scene may intentionally differ.

Use warm/cool balance, value separation, saturation, texture and localized treatment as controls. Keep source-specific colors, crop, borders and treatment values in the project. A pleasing warm subject against a cooler setting does not prescribe one LUT, one hue pair or a universal skin transform. A grayscale or washed transition is a scoped expressive choice; it is not permission to lose important information.

Inspect the actual encoded result, particularly moving backgrounds behind text and the boundaries between sources. Prefer fixing the source or layout problem before adding an opaque panel, heavy shadow or more effects.

## Invent through function and variation

Describe a learned relationship independently from its surface form. For example, a phrase may become a small visual composition whose most meaningful part receives emphasis. Realize that relationship through a serif title, a restrained sans caption, a diagram, handwriting, a typographic environment or another suitable construction. Choose from intent rather than a menu of mandatory presets.

When a decision is uncertain, vary an independent dimension such as text role, medium, composition, pacing character or emphasis channel. Compare only the alternatives needed to resolve it. There is no quota of variants, preferred genre, required reference, permanent color code or minimum movement. Retain a simpler option when it serves the message; explore a more ambitious one when the construction and tools can support it.

## Review and integrate

Judge clarity, character, emotional intention and continuity separately from export success. Inspect source facts and graphic meaning; review text at delivery size; inspect movement and cuts at target speed; listen before claiming speech or music alignment. Sampled frames establish visible states and their order, not actual perceived rhythm or sound relationships.

Route a failure to the smallest responsible owner. Transcript changes return to alignment before emphasis; a font change returns to typography before layout; retiming returns to the shared time map before motion; a crop changes projected text and tracking evidence; a grade may invalidate text contrast. Preserve unrelated approved choices. Keep the general lesson provisional when its intended audience effect has not been tested.
